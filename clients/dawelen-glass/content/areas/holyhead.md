## Exposure on Holy Island

Holyhead has the most weather-beaten housing stock on Anglesey. The town is
almost surrounded by water, and the prevailing wind arrives with nothing in
front of it, so glazing here ages faster than anywhere else we work: seals
fail early, and the units that fail are almost always the ones facing the
sea.

That pattern is useful, because it means the repair can be targeted. There is
rarely any point replacing glass on the sheltered side of a house at the same
time.

## Older terraces, awkward openings

Much of the town centre and the streets above the harbour is Victorian and
Edwardian terracing, some of it with early uPVC fitted into openings that
have moved since. Glass cut to a nominal size does not go in. Every unit gets
measured in the rebate as it is now.

## The port, the rentals and the shops

Holyhead has a larger share of rented housing and small commercial frontage
than the rest of the island. Both come with obligations: a tenanted property
with broken glass needs making safe quickly, and a shopfront pane usually has
to be toughened or laminated rather than simply replaced like for like.
