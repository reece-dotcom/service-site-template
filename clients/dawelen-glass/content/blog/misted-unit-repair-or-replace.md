Condensation between the panes is the most common glazing complaint we get,
and it is also the one where people are most often sold far more than they
need. Here is how to work out which job you actually have.

## The test is the frame, not the glass

The misting itself tells you one thing only: the perimeter seal on the
sealed unit has failed. It says nothing about the frame. So the question is
whether the frame is still doing its job.

Go and look at the window with the door open, and check three things:

**Does it close and lock cleanly?** Hardware can be adjusted and replaced.
Hinges and handles are not a reason to scrap a frame.

**Is the frame itself sound?** On timber, press the bottom of the frame and
the cill with your thumb — soft or spongy means rot, and rot means the frame
is the problem. On uPVC, look for distortion, cracking at the welded corners,
and whether the sashes still sit square in the opening.

**Is water getting in around the frame rather than through it?** Failed
pointing or perished mastic outside is a separate repair, and worth sorting
whichever way you go.

If the frame is sound, you need glass. If it is not, you need a window.

## What a sealed unit swap involves

The beads come out, the failed unit comes out, the new one goes in on new
packers and tape, and the beads go back. Under an hour, per unit, once the
glass has been made. No making good, no redecorating, no scaffolding.

The wait is in the glass, not the fitting: every unit is cut to the
measurement taken off your window.

## Why coastal units fail early

On this island, exposure decides everything. A north or east-facing
elevation takes salt, driven rain and sustained wind pressure, and perimeter
seals give up years earlier than they do on a sheltered wall. It is normal
here to find the seaward side of a house completely misted while the
sheltered side is perfectly clear.

That pattern is worth pointing out, because it is often used the other way
around — as evidence that "the windows have had it" and the whole house
needs doing.

## When replacing the window really is right

- The frame is rotten, distorted or has dropped.
- The window is single glazed and you want that to change.
- The opening has to change: a window becoming a door, or an opening
  enlarged.
- The frame is a style that has no serviceable hardware left.

In those cases new glass into an old frame is money spent twice, and we will
say so.

## Before you accept a quote

Ask which of the two jobs you are being quoted for, and why. A quote for
replacement windows that never mentions the option of a sealed unit swap has
not answered the question — and on a house where only one elevation has
misted, the difference between the two is usually thousands of pounds.
