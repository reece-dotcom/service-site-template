## First job: make it safe

Broken glass is a safety and security problem before it is a glazing problem.
Someone has to clear the shards, get the remaining glass out of the frame
without it dropping, and cover the opening so the building is not standing
open.

Only after that does it become a question of what glass goes back in.

## What goes back in

Not all of it is ordinary glass. Low-level glazing, doors and the panels
beside them, and anything in a bathroom have to meet the impact-safety
requirements in Approved Document K, which in practice means toughened or
laminated glass. If the original was toughened, the replacement has to be.

On holiday lets and rentals that matters twice over: it is the landlord's
liability, and a cheap pane in a door side panel is exactly the thing that
gets noticed after an accident rather than before.

## Around the island

Wind is the other common cause here. A gate or a bin taken up in a gale
against a patio door does the same damage as a break-in, and tends to happen
to the whole street on the same night — so tell us when you ring whether the
property is occupied, and we will work out what needs doing first.
