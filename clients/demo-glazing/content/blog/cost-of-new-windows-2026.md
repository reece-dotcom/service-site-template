Every glazing company dodges this question, so here's a straight answer.

## The price ranges

For a standard three-bed semi with eight to ten openings, most Greater
Manchester households in 2026 land somewhere between £6,000 and £11,000
for A-rated uPVC casements, supplied and fitted. Flush casements add
roughly 15–20%. Sliding sash roughly doubles it. Aluminium sits above uPVC
but below sash.

That's a wide range because the variables are genuinely wide.

## What actually moves the price

**Opening sizes.** A large bay is not one window, it's three or four plus
structural support.

**Glass specification.** Acoustic glass on a main road, toughened where
building regs require it, obscured in bathrooms. Each adds cost per unit.

**Making good.** If the reveals are rotten or a lintel has dropped, that
work is real and it isn't free.

**Access.** Second-floor windows over a conservatory roof need scaffold.

## The tactic to walk away from

If a salesperson quotes £14,000, then phones "the manager" and comes down
to £7,000 before the tea's cold, the £7,000 was the price all along. The
discount is theatre. A company confident in its pricing gives you one
number and leaves.

## What to ask for

Ask for the price in writing, itemised, with the frame system named, the
glass specification stated, and scaffold and making-good either included or
explicitly excluded. Any firm that won't put that on paper is telling you
something useful.
