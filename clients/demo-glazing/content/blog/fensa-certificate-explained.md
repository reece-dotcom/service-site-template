Replacement windows and doors are notifiable under Building Regulations.
Someone has to certify the work meets them. That's either your local
building control department — slow, and you pay a fee — or a registered
installer self-certifying through a competent person scheme like FENSA.

## Why it matters later

You will not think about this again until you sell the house. Then your
buyer's solicitor asks for the certificate for the windows fitted in 2019,
and if there isn't one you're buying indemnity insurance or renegotiating
the sale price at the worst possible moment.

## What to check before you buy

Ask for the installer's FENSA registration number and check it on the FENSA
website yourself. "We're FENSA approved" on a van means nothing without a
number behind it.

## If your certificate never arrived

Registration should happen within 30 days of the work. If you're past that,
contact the installer first. If they've stopped trading, FENSA can search
their records by address — the work may well have been registered even if
the paperwork never reached you. Failing that, your council can issue a
regularisation certificate, which costs money but resolves the sale.
