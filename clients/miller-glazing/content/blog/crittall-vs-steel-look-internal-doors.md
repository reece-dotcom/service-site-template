"Crittall doors" has become the phrase people use for any black-framed glazed
door with horizontal bars. It is worth knowing what you are actually being
quoted for, because two products share the look and they are not the same
price.

## Crittall is a brand

Crittall Windows is an Essex company — it has been in Braintree and then
Witham for over a century — and it makes genuine hot-rolled steel windows,
doors and screens. Their internal door and screen range is steel, welded,
galvanised and polyester-powder-coated. It is the original, and the
sightlines are as slim as it gets.

## Steel-look is a style

Most of what is fitted in houses today is aluminium made to look like steel:
the same black finish, the same proportions, the same grid of horizontal
bars. Aluminium extrusions are slightly deeper than steel sections, so a
trained eye sees a marginally chunkier frame, but at normal viewing
distance the effect is the same.

## Which should you choose indoors?

For an internal door or screen the honest answer is usually aluminium:

- **Weight.** A steel French pair is heavy. Aluminium hangs on lighter
  hinges, is easier to fit into a domestic wall and is kinder to the floor
  if a leaf ever drops.
- **Cost.** Genuine steel is a premium product with a lead time to match.
  Aluminium is typically a good deal less for the same opening.
- **Maintenance.** Powder-coated aluminium does not rust. Steel is
  galvanised and coated, and indoors that is fine, but a chip near a wet
  room is worth touching up.
- **Thermal performance.** Irrelevant indoors, which is the point: the
  thermal break that matters on an external steel-look door does not
  matter on a hall door.

Where genuine steel wins is authenticity: a house that still has its
original 1930s Crittall windows, and an owner who wants the internal screen
to match exactly.
