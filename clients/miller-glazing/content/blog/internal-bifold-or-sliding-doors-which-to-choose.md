Once a kitchen has been opened into the room behind it, the question is how
to close it again when you want to. The two sensible answers are a bifold
and a slider, and they suit different rooms.

## How much of the opening do you want clear?

A **bifold** folds its leaves back against one reveal (or splits and stacks
both ways) and clears almost the whole width. If the reason you knocked the
wall down was to have one big space at weekends, this is the door.

A **slider** only ever opens half of the width, because one pane has to sit
behind the other. If you mostly want to see through rather than walk
through, that is no loss at all.

## How much room do you have either side?

Bifold leaves stack at right angles to the wall, so a four-leaf set needs
roughly the depth of one leaf clear beside the opening. Sliders need no
depth at all, which is why they win in a corridor, a galley kitchen or a
room where a sofa sits against the wall next to the opening.

## What about the floor?

Our sliders are top-hung: the weight is carried on a track in the head and
there is nothing on the floor. Bifolds also hang from the top and use a slim
bottom guide, which sits flush with most floors. Neither needs a channel
cut into your finish.

## Glass

Both take the same glass. Grey or bronze tint softens the view of a working
kitchen without blocking light; reeded or satin glass hides it completely.
The bronze-tinted sliders in our gallery were chosen precisely so the
kitchen could be untidy while the living room stayed calm.

## What we would fit in your house

- Opening wider than about 2.4 m and you want it fully open: **bifold**.
- Tight room, furniture against the wall, or a corridor: **slider**.
- You mainly want light and a view: **slider**, or a fixed screen with a
  single door.
