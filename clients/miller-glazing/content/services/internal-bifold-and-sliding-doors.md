## The knocked-through kitchen problem

A lot of the houses we work in have had the kitchen opened into the dining
or living room — it is the most common alteration in the 1930s semis and
terraces across Dagenham, Romford and Ilford. The result is a lovely bright
space for most of the day and a room that fills with cooking smell and
dishwasher noise the moment someone sits down to watch television.

A glazed bifold or slider across that opening gives you both. Open, the
rooms read as one. Closed, the light still comes through and the noise
mostly does not.

## Bifolds

Three to six leaves on a top track, folding flat to one side or split so the
stack lands where you want it. The four-leaf set in our gallery folds to one
reveal and leaves the opening clear.

Handles and hinges are black to match the frame; glass is toughened as
standard.

## Sliders

Internal sliding doors run as two or three panes on a top-hung track, so nothing sits on the floor. The
sliders in our gallery use bronze-tinted glass, which is a good compromise
in a house where the kitchen is the working room and the living room is the
tidy one.

## Fitting

Measured by us, made to the opening, fitted by us. Where the structural
opening is being formed by a builder, we measure once it is in and plastered
rather than off a drawing — that is how you get the frame tight to the
reveal. For single doors and fixed screens, see
