A double glazed unit is two panes of glass with a spacer bar between them, a
drying agent inside that bar, and a seal round the edge holding it all
together. The drying agent absorbs any moisture in the cavity.

## What has gone wrong

The edge seal has failed. Air with moisture in it now moves in and out of the
cavity, and once the drying agent is saturated it can't do anything about it.
On a cold morning that moisture condenses on the inside of the outer pane —
and you can't reach it.

## Why you can't clean it

The mist is on the internal faces of the sealed unit. Drilling and "draining"
a unit is sold occasionally as a cheap fix; it removes the visible fog for a
while but the cavity is now vented, so it will haze again and the insulation
value is gone for good.

## What the repair involves

The beading comes off, the failed unit comes out, the new unit goes in on
fresh packers and the beads go back. The frame, hinges and handles all stay.
It's typically under an hour per window once the glass has arrived.

## Stopping the next one

Blocked drainage slots at the bottom of the frame leave the unit sitting in
water, which is what kills the seal in the first place. Clearing them takes a
minute and it's worth doing on every window in the house, not just the one
that has failed.
