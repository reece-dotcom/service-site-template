Almost every "free survey" you book with a replacement company ends the same
way: you need new windows. That's not a conspiracy, it's just what happens
when the person diagnosing the problem only sells one cure.

## Repair it if

- **The glass has misted.** The seal has failed, not the window. Swap the unit.
- **The handle spins or won't lock.** That's hardware — a mechanism, a gearbox
  or a cam, all replaceable in place.
- **It's stiff to open.** Usually a corroded friction stay. Cheap fix.
- **The door needs lifting and shoving.** It has dropped on its hinges. Adjust
  it before the gearbox snaps.

## Replace it if

- **The frame itself has gone.** uPVC that has become brittle and cracked, or
  timber rotted through at the cill, is past hardware repair.
- **The frame is out of square because the opening has moved.** No amount of
  adjustment fixes a structural problem.
- **You're changing the house.** New opening sizes, a different style, or going
  from single glazing throughout — that's a replacement job.

## The number that matters

A sealed unit or a door mechanism is a small job. A house of new windows is
thousands. If the frames are sound, spending the larger number buys you very
little that the smaller number doesn't.
